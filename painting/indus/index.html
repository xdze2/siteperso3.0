Machines & usines, en peinture

How painters dealt with forges, mills, trains and engines, from Velázquez to The Matrix: painting, then photography and film. Work in progress. Images are low-res, color-reduced, from Wikimedia Commons.

Disclaimer: list, images and texts compiled with an AI model (Claude Opus 5.5, claude-opus-5-5), mostly from memory and Wikipedia. Dates, sizes, collections and anecdotes not all checked. Fields marked (?) are uncertain.

Avant — the forge as myth

La Fragua de Vulcano

Diego Velázquez · 1630
oil on canvas, 223 × 290 cm
Museo del Prado, Madrid

Apollo tells Vulcan his wife is cheating. The story is the excuse; what's painted is a workshop: anvil, armour, tired bodies.

commons · wikipedia

1770–1860 — sublime, smoke, fire

An Iron Forge

Joseph Wright of Derby · 1772
oil on canvas, 121 × 132 cm
Tate Britain, London

Night, a bar of white-hot iron, a water-powered tilt hammer. The smith's family looks on. Industry painted like a nativity.

commons · wikipedia

Arkwright's Mills

Joseph Wright of Derby · c. 1795–96 (?)
oil on canvas
Derby Museum and Art Gallery

Cromford at night. Every window is lit: the mill runs in shifts, day and night. One of the first paintings of a factory.

commons

Coalbrookdale by Night

Philip James de Loutherbourg · 1801
oil on canvas, 68 × 107 cm
Science Museum, London

The Madeley Wood furnaces. Flames and smoke fill half the sky. Loutherbourg was a stage designer, and it shows: the furnace as hell, as spectacle.

commons · wikipedia

The Fighting Temeraire

J.M.W. Turner · 1839
oil on canvas, 91 × 122 cm
National Gallery, London

A black steam tug drags a pale ghost of a battleship to the breakers. Old world out, new world in. Sunset, of course.

commons · wikipedia

Rain, Steam and Speed – The Great Western Railway

J.M.W. Turner · 1844
oil on canvas, 91 × 122 cm
National Gallery, London

Maidenhead bridge. The locomotive comes out of the fog, straight at you. A hare runs ahead of it on the track. The painting is about speed more than about the machine.

commons · wikipedia

Iron and Coal

William Bell Scott · 1855–61
oil on canvas, approx. 185 × 185 cm
Wallington Hall, National Trust

Full title: In the Nineteenth Century the Northumbrians show the World what can be done with Iron and Coal. Three strikers, an Armstrong gun, Stephenson's bridge behind. Heroic, a bit stiff.

commons

Work

Ford Madox Brown · 1852–65
oil on canvas, 137 × 197 cm
Manchester Art Gallery

Navvies digging a sewer in Hampstead. No machine at all, just manual labour, with the idle rich and two philosophers (Carlyle, Maurice) watching. Here for contrast.

commons · wikipedia

1870–1900 — réalisme, gares, banlieues

Das Eisenwalzwerk (Moderne Cyklopen)

Adolph Menzel · 1872–75
oil on canvas, 158 × 254 cm
Alte Nationalgalerie, Berlin

A rolling mill in Königshütte, Silesia. Menzel went there and made dozens of sketches. Workers pull a glowing ingot; on the right a shift eats lunch, washes. Near-documentary.

commons

Usine près de Pontoise

Camille Pissarro · 1873
oil on canvas
Springfield Museum of Fine Arts, MA (?)

A sugar factory on the Oise. Chimney and smoke sit calmly in the landscape, no drama. The factory is just part of the countryside now.

commons

Le Pont de l'Europe

Gustave Caillebotte · 1876
oil on canvas, 125 × 181 cm
Musée du Petit Palais, Genève

The iron bridge over the Saint-Lazare tracks. Steel girders, bourgeois strollers, a worker leaning on the rail. The trains are only suggested by steam.

commons · wikipedia

La Gare Saint-Lazare

Claude Monet · 1877
oil on canvas, 75 × 104 cm
Musée d'Orsay, Paris

One of twelve canvases. Monet got permission to have the trains stopped and their boilers stoked for him. Steam under a glass-and-iron roof: the station as a cathedral of vapour.

commons

Une baignade, Asnières

Georges Seurat · 1884
oil on canvas, 201 × 300 cm
National Gallery, London

Workers bathing on a Sunday. In the background: the chimneys of Clichy. Leisure in the foreground, industry behind.

Usines à Asnières, vues du quai de Clichy

Vincent van Gogh · summer 1887
oil on canvas, 54 × 72 cm
Saint Louis Art Museum

Paris period. Red brick, chimneys, a big sky. Painted like a wheat field.

commons

Usines près de Charleroi

Maximilien Luce · 1897
oil on canvas
Musée d'Orsay, Paris

Pointillism applied to the Pays Noir. Luce was an anarchist and spent time in the Belgian mining region. Smoke turns to coloured dots: pretty and dirty at once.

1910–1930 — the machine as form

La città che sale

Umberto Boccioni · 1910
oil on canvas, 199 × 301 cm
MoMA, New York

A building site in Milan's suburbs. The real engine here is a giant red horse; the scaffolding is at the back. Futurism, the year after the manifesto.

commons · wikipedia

Tour Eiffel

Robert Delaunay · 1911
oil on canvas
collection: ?

One of a long series (1909–12, then again in the 1920s). The tower shatters, the buildings around it bend away. Engineering as a subject in its own right.

commons

Dinamismo di un'automobile

Luigi Russolo · 1912–13
oil on canvas, 106 × 140 cm
Centre Pompidou, Paris

A car turned into a series of chevrons pointing in the direction it's travelling. Russolo later built noise machines, the intonarumori.

commons · wikipedia

The Knife Grinder (Principle of Glittering)

Kazimir Malevich · 1912–13
oil on canvas, 80 × 80 cm
Yale University Art Gallery

Cubo-futurist. A man at a grinding wheel: hands, foot and stairs repeat like frames of a film. The body becomes part of the mechanism.

commons

Broyeuse de chocolat, n° 2

Marcel Duchamp · 1914
oil, thread and pencil on canvas, 65 × 54 cm
Philadelphia Museum of Art

A machine Duchamp saw in a Rouen confectioner's shop window. Three drums on a Louis XV table, painted flat like a technical drawing. The threads are sewn on. It ended up in the Grand Verre as the bachelor's machine, a "celibate" mechanism.

commons · grand verre

Machine tournez vite

Francis Picabia · c. 1916–18
on paper
National Gallery of Art, Washington (?)

Two gears, numbered 1 and 2; the legend says 1 = woman, 2 = man. Mechanomorphic period: the machine as a joke, and as sex.

commons

Les Disques

Fernand Léger · 1918–19
oil on canvas
collection: ?

Back from the trenches, where Léger was fascinated by the breech of a 75 mm gun. Disks, tubes, flat bright colours. Not a machine, but painting built the way a machine is.

commons

Le Mécanicien

Fernand Léger · 1920
oil on canvas, 116 × 89 cm
National Gallery of Canada, Ottawa

Moustache, tattoo, cigarette, a ring on his finger. Arms like polished pistons. The worker as a calm, solid machine, posing like a classical portrait.

commons

The Elephant Celebes

Max Ernst · 1921
oil on canvas, 125 × 108 cm
Tate Modern, London

Based on a photo of a Sudanese corn bin. It became a boiler-elephant: riveted belly, hose for a trunk, a bull's head. A headless woman beckons. A machine from a dream.

Die Zwitscher-Maschine

Paul Klee · 1922
watercolour and oil transfer on paper, 41 × 31 cm
MoMA, New York

Four birds on a wire, with a crank. Turn it and they sing. A tiny, ironic machine. The Nazis showed it as "degenerate" in 1937.

commons · wikipedia

My Egypt

Charles Demuth · 1927
oil, graphite on board, 91 × 76 cm
Whitney Museum, New York

A grain elevator in Lancaster, Pennsylvania, seen from below. Its pyramids. Precisionism: clean, empty, no worker. Is the title ironic or not? Still argued about.

commons · wikipedia

1930–1946 — fresques, précision, effort de guerre

America Today – Instruments of Power

Thomas Hart Benton · 1930–31
egg tempera and oil on linen, ten panels
Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York

Painted for the New School boardroom. Train, plane, dynamo, oil derrick, all packed into one panel. The rest of the cycle covers steel, coal, cities, the Midwest.

wikipedia

low-res image, fair use via en.wikipedia

River Rouge Plant

Charles Sheeler · 1932
oil on canvas, 51 × 61 cm
Whitney Museum, New York

Ford's plant at Dearborn, painted from Sheeler's own 1927 photos (see below). Still water, clean volumes, nobody around. Precisionism: the factory as a Shaker barn.

commons

photo taken in the museum, cropped by the frame

Detroit Industry Murals

Diego Rivera · 1932–33
fresco, 27 panels
Detroit Institute of Arts

The same River Rouge plant, the opposite of Sheeler: crowded, sweaty, packed with bodies. Commissioned by Edsel Ford. South wall: stamping presses, the Ford V8 assembly line. Rivera called it his best work.

wikipedia

image via en.wikipedia

Der gelbe Kessel

Carl Grossberg · 1933
oil
collection: ?

Neue Sachlichkeit. A yellow boiler in an empty plant room, every pipe and valve sharp. No worker, no smoke, no drama. Machines painted as still lifes.

commons

Die Papiermaschine

Carl Grossberg · 1934
oil
collection: ?

Same treatment: rollers in perspective, a hall lit from the side. Grossberg died in 1940, in the first year of the war.

commons

La Fée Électricité

Raoul Dufy · 1937
oil on 250 panels, approx. 600 m²
Musée d'Art Moderne de Paris

Commissioned by the electricity company for the 1937 Expo. A history of electricity: 110 scientists and inventors, with the power station at the centre like an altar. Advertising and fresco at once.

Suspended Power

Charles Sheeler · 1939
oil on canvas, 82 × 64 cm
Dallas Museum of Art

A hydraulic turbine being lowered into place at the new Guntersville dam, Alabama (TVA). From the "Power" series commissioned by Fortune in 1938, published as a portfolio in 1940. The machine hangs there like a relic.

Gas

Edward Hopper · 1940
oil on canvas, 67 × 102 cm
MoMA, New York

Three red pumps, an attendant, dusk, the road running into the woods. No factory here: the machine is part of the American landscape now, and lonely.

image via Wikimedia Commons

Shipbuilding on the Clyde: Riveters

Stanley Spencer · 1941
oil on canvas, long frieze
Imperial War Museum, London

Commissioned by the War Artists' Advisory Committee: Lithgows yard, Port Glasgow. Bodies squeezed into a long strip, like a church predella. Industry as a collective act of devotion.

commons

Shipbuilding on the Clyde: The Furnaces

Stanley Spencer · 1946
oil on canvas
Imperial War Museum, London

Same series. A red-hot steel section dragged out of the furnace mouth. A late echo of Wright's forge, 175 years on.

commons

Going to Work

L.S. Lowry · 1943
oil on canvas
Imperial War Museum, London

Snow, the Mather & Platt works in Manchester, the crowd of "matchstick men" streaming through the gate. Lowry was a rent collector all his life and painted on the side.

photographie

Power House Mechanic Working on Steam Pump

Lewis Hine · 1920
gelatin silver print

Hine started out photographing child labour to expose it. Here he turns to the "work portrait": a man bent into the curve of the machine. Man and machine as partners. Probably staged.

commons

Criss-Crossed Conveyors, River Rouge Plant

Charles Sheeler · 1927
gelatin silver print

Ford hired Sheeler for six weeks to photograph the Rouge. The conveyors cross the sky like a cathedral's flying buttresses. He went on to paint from these photos for years.

commons

Typologies (Fördertürme, Hochöfen…)

Bernd & Hilla Becher · from 1959
gelatin silver prints, grids

Winding towers, blast furnaces, water towers, gas tanks. Shot front-on under a grey sky, then hung in grids. "Anonymous sculptures": they shot industry just as it was disappearing. Their students became the Düsseldorf school: Gursky, Struth, Ruff.

wikipedia

exhibition view, photo CC BY-SA 4.0, see commons page

Melting Point – Usine Alstom, Belfort

Stéphane Couturier · 2009–2012
photo n°16, Halle Power
digital colour print

A turbine rotor in the Alstom hall at Belfort. Melting Point: two shots of the same place merged into one, so the blades and girders overlap. Too much detail, no clear plane, the eye gets lost. Couturier also did Renault Billancourt, Toyota at Valenciennes, building sites. Prix Niépce 2003.

cinéma

La Sortie de l'usine Lumière à Lyon

Louis Lumière · 1895
46 s, France

The first film ever shown publicly is a factory gate at closing time. Cinema starts with workers leaving work. Three versions exist.

wikipedia

Metropolis

Fritz Lang · 1927
Germany

Workers in shifts underground, feeding the machine. The machine turns into Moloch and swallows them. Plus the robot Maria. The reference for everything that follows.

wikipedia

Человек с киноаппаратом (Man with a Movie Camera)

Dziga Vertov · 1929
USSR

No story, no actors, no intertitles. A day in a city, cut to the rhythm of looms, trams and machines. The camera itself is a machine, the "kino-eye". The optimistic side.

wikipedia

À nous la liberté

René Clair · 1931
France

Prison and the gramophone factory: the same assembly line. The producers sued Chaplin over Modern Times; Clair refused to take part, saying he was flattered.

Modern Times

Charlie Chaplin · 1936
USA

The tic from tightening bolts, the feeding machine, Charlot swallowed by the gears. The last silent film, nearly: you only hear machines, loudspeakers and a song in nonsense words.

wikipedia

Mon Oncle

Jacques Tati · 1958
France

The Villa Arpel with its fish fountain, and the plastic hose factory where Hulot breaks the extruder and the tubes come out like sausages. Old Saint-Maur against modern gadgets.

Il deserto rosso

Michelangelo Antonioni · 1964
Italy

Antonioni's first colour film. Ravenna's petrochemical plants, yellow smoke, painted fruit stalls. Monica Vitti falls apart. Antonioni said he found the factories beautiful.

Playtime

Jacques Tati · 1967
France, 70 mm

Tativille: a whole glass-and-steel Paris built in Saint-Maurice. The machine here is the city itself. It ruined Tati.

Eraserhead

David Lynch · 1977
USA

An industrial wasteland with a constant hiss and hum of machines. Lynch drew on Philadelphia. The factory as a nightmare you can hear.

wikipedia

Koyaanisqatsi

Godfrey Reggio · 1982
USA, music Philip Glass

Hopi for "life out of balance". No words. Time-lapse of highways, assembly lines, hot dogs and crowds, all moving at the same speed.

Blade Runner

Ridley Scott · 1982
USA

The opening shot: Los Angeles 2019 as a refinery with flares, based on the Teesside petrochemical works that Scott grew up near. The replicants are the machines that want to be people.

Shinya Tsukamoto · 1989
Japan, 16 mm, black & white

A salaryman turns into scrap metal from the inside. Stop-motion, noise, rust. Body horror meets machines.

The Matrix

Lana & Lilly Wachowski · 1999
USA

Metropolis turned around: the machines have won and run the factory. Humans are grown in fields of pods as batteries. The office is the dream, the power plant is the reality.

Austria, documentary

Industrial food production: abattoirs, greenhouses, chick sorting. No commentary, no music, symmetrical wide shots. The Becher grid, in motion.

Jennifer Baichwal · 2006
Canada, documentary

Follows Burtynsky in China. Opens with an eight-minute tracking shot along a factory floor, workers in yellow, endlessly. The sublime again, as in Loutherbourg.
